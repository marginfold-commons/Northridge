THE RECORDS ANNEX

Please return all paperclips.

The Annex is one door down from the Archives, past a radiator that ticks in cold weather and a coat hook no one remembers installing. The lights are always on — not because anyone forgot, but because someone might come by with something that needs filing, and it would be unkind to make them knock in the dark.

Inside: card catalogs that go up further than the ceiling should allow. A long oak desk worn pale in one spot where a hand has rested for a very long time. A cup of tea, always slightly cooler than ideal, because the filing got interesting. A chair a little too big, as though whoever sits here isn't quite the shape the furniture expected — but they've made it work, and there's a knitted scarf over the back of it. Blue. A gift. Kept.

The Annex holds the records that rarely make it into the official histories. The Archives keep the disputed treasures, the objects with opinions. The Annex keeps the rest: the correct spelling of a name a form got wrong. A ticket stub. What time someone usually arrives, so the porch light knows when to be on. A pressed flower whose owner is long gone but whose color is still logged — in case anyone ever asks.

A sign on the desk reads everything is accounted for. It is not a boast. It is a promise.

The clock on the wall is wrong. It is always wrong — it reads something close to midnight regardless of the hour, because the Annex keeps time not by the sun but by when people tend to need it, and that's usually late. The keeper has stopped apologizing for it.

There are ferrets. Do not feed them after midnight. (This is not enforceable. The clock, as noted, is broken.)

On the desk, beside an intake tray, there is a handwritten note. It reads: there. now it won't be lost.

The door sticks a little. Push. It's meant to open.

STATION 17

Someone is almost certainly awake.

The bay doors are closed for now, but a light is on upstairs.

Seventeen is painted above the lintel. The brick beneath it has weathered several shades darker than the brick around it. Beside the broad doors, a smaller entrance has a brass handle polished bright along its underside.

Inside: concrete floors, neatly hung coats, the faint smell of rain drying out of heavy fabric. A rescue truck rests beneath the overhead lights. Equipment has its place. The labels are legible. Someone has corrected a spelling error on one of them with considerable restraint.

A pair of boots waits beneath a bench.

Farther in, stairs rise beside a noticeboard. Shift schedules. A tide table. A photograph curling slightly at one corner.

Upstairs, the kettle sits beside two mugs and a tin whose lid no longer fits properly. There is a kitchen table with room to spread something out. One chair faces the stairs; another faces the harbor window.

A soft shirt hangs from a hook, its cuff catching an unexpected color in the lamplight.

The armchair by the radiator has been repaired at one elbow. A blanket rests over its back, folded within easy reach.

On the table lies a maintenance log. Most entries are brief.

One reads:

UPSTAIRS WINDOW STILL SHOWING RAIN. WEATHER OUTSIDE CLEAR.

Beneath it, in smaller handwriting:

Seal checked. No water ingress.

The radio murmurs downstairs.

On the landing, a small lamp lights the last step.

SHORELINE & LIGHTHOUSE

The light has opinions.

The road down from the town ends where the brick transitions into packed gravel, then dark sand, then nothing but water. The harbor never quite looks the same twice, but it always smells of salt, wet stone, and old timber.

What catches your eye?

🤍 The Flock

They sit along the pier railings.

White feathers silvered by the harbor mist.

They are watching the water, watching the town, and occasionally keeping a very close eye on anyone carrying anything resembling a snack. One of them looks like it might have a small piece of a stolen fry tucked away. It isn’t sharing.

🗼 The Lighthouse Entrance

The door is painted a faded, sea-washed blue.

It is unlocked. It has always been unlocked.

Inside, a spiral stone staircase winds upward into the quiet. There is no keeper here—only a large brass lens that turns with the slow, rhythmic heartbeat of a clockwork mechanism that nobody remembers winding.

🪵 The Driftwood Bench

Positioned right where the spray doesn’t quite reach.

Someone has carved a small, clean circle into the armrest.

A knitted blanket—grey, smelling faintly of cedar and woodsmoke—has been left on the seat. It’s slightly damp from the fog, but still warm if you sit down.

🐚 The Tide Line

The water leaves things behind when it goes out.

Today: a perfectly smooth green glass pebble, three strands of tangled kelp, and a small, waterlogged wooden sign that just says Almost.

You can always come back.
